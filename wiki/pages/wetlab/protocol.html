LB liquid/solid medium

Equipment

  • Magnetic stirrer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA)
  • Autoclave sterilizer (Jiangyin Binjiang Medical Equipment Co., Ltd., Jiangyin, China)

All microbial culture experiments in this study adopted autoclaved media.

Reagents
Reagent Amount (per 1 L)
Tryptone10 g
Yeast extract5 g
NaCl10 g
Procedure
  1. Add the above reagents to a graduated container. Then, add RO water until the total volume reaches 1 L.
  2. Stir on a magnetic stirrer until the solutes are completely dissolved.
  3. Aliquot the liquid into several conical flasks.
  4. Sterilize the medium in an autoclave at 121℃ for 20 minutes at a pressure of 15 psi (1.05kg/cm²).

Note: 15 g/L Agar powder was added for LB solid medium.

LK kanamycin‑resistant liquid/solid medium

Equipment

  • Magnetic stirrer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA)
  • Autoclave sterilizer (Jiangyin Binjiang Medical Equipment Co., Ltd., Jiangyin, China)
Reagents
Reagent Amount (per 1 L)
Tryptone10 g
Yeast extract5 g
NaCl10 g
Kanamycin50 mg
Procedure
  1. Prepare LB liquid medium following the protocol above, then autoclave at 121℃ for 20 min and cool it to 50–60 ℃.
  2. Add kanamycin stock solution to the cooled sterile medium to a final concentration of 50 mg/L under aseptic conditions.
  3. Mix gently, then aliquot into tubes or pour into plates, for screening and scale‑up culture of plasmid‑bearing engineered strains.

Note: For solid LK plates, add 15 g/L agar powder before autoclaving.

2.1 Plasmid Extraction

Equipment:

  • Centrifuge (Eppendorf, Germany)
  • Vortex mixer (Dragon‑Lab, Beijing, China)
  • Pipettor (Eppendorf, Germany)
  • Thermostatic oven (Hengyi, Shanghai, China)

Reagents (from commercial plasmid extraction kit)

RS* buffer (containing RNase A)、LB lysis buffer、BD binding buffer、AC buffer、PL* deproteinization buffer、W* wash buffer、Nuclease‑free sterile water / elution buffer

Procedure

  1. Take 3 mL overnight‑grown bacterial culture, centrifuge at 12 000 rpm for 30 s, completely discard supernatant, and collect the cell pellet.
  2. Add 250 μL pre‑chilled RS* buffer containing RNase A. Pipette repeatedly or vortex to fully resuspend cells without clumping, in order to remove RNA impurities.
  3. Add 250 μL LB lysis buffer. Gently invert the tube 6‑8 times and incubate at room temperature for 4 min to achieve thorough cell lysis.
  4. Immediately add 350 μL BD binding buffer. Gently invert the tube 6‑8 times for full mixing, incubate at room temperature for 2 min, centrifuge at 13 000 rpm for 10 min, and carefully collect the supernatant.
  5. Add 100 μL AC buffer to the P3 DNA adsorption column, centrifuge at 13 000 rpm for 1 min, and discard the waste liquid.
  6. Transfer centrifuged supernatant onto the pretreated adsorption column. Centrifuge at 12 000 rpm for 1 min to allow full binding of plasmid DNA to the column membrane, and discard waste liquid.
  7. Add 500 μL PL* deproteinization buffer, centrifuge at 12 000 rpm for 1 min, and discard waste liquid.
  8. Place the P3 DNA adsorption column back into collection tube. Add 600 μL W* wash buffer and centrifuge at 12 000 rpm at room temperature for 30 s; discard waste liquid. Repeat the washing step once to thoroughly remove residual salts and ethanol impurities.
  9. Return P3 DNA adsorption column to collection tube. Centrifuge the empty column at 12 000 rpm for 2 min to remove residual wash buffer. Open the column lid and bake in an oven for 10 min to completely evaporate residual ethanol.
  10. Transfer the adsorption column into a brand‑new sterile 1.5 mL centrifuge tube. Add 30 μL nuclease‑free sterile water or elution buffer dropwise onto the centre of column membrane. Incubate at room temperature for 2 min, then centrifuge at 12 000 rpm for 1 min to elute pure plasmid DNA. Store at −20 °C for subsequent use.

2.2 Recovery of DNA Products

Equipment:

  • Centrifuge (Eppendorf, Germany)
  • Pipettor (Eppendorf, Germany)
  • Thermostatic oven (Hengyi, Shanghai, China)

Reagents (from commercial DNA purification kit)

GDP binding buffer、GW wash buffer (supplemented with anhydrous ethanol)、Elution Buffer、Sterile deionized water (dd H₂O)

Procedure

  1. Adjust the volume of DNA sample to be purified to 100 μL with sterile deionized water in a sterile 2 mL centrifuge tube. Add a 5‑fold volume of GDP binding buffer and invert for thorough mixing.
  2. Briefly centrifuge to collect droplets on tube wall. Place FastPure DNA Mini Columns‑G adsorption column into a 2 mL Collection Tube. Transfer 600 μL sample solution onto the adsorption column and centrifuge at 12 000 rpm (13 800 × g) for 30‑60 sec.
  3. Discard flow‑through. Add 300 μL GDP buffer to the adsorption column, incubate at room temperature for 1 min, centrifuge at 12 000 rpm for 60 sec.
  4. Discard flow‑through. Add 700 μL GW buffer supplemented with anhydrous ethanol onto the adsorption column, centrifuge at 12 000 rpm for 60 sec and discard waste liquid. Repeat washing once to completely eliminate salt residues that may interfere with downstream experiments.
  5. Centrifuge empty adsorption column at 12 000 rpm for 2 min. Open lid and bake at low temperature for less than 10 min to fully remove residual ethanol.
  6. Place adsorption column into a sterilized 1.5 mL centrifuge tube. Add 20‑30 μL pre‑warmed (55 °C) elution buffer / dd H₂O onto the centre of column membrane. Incubate at room temperature for 2 min, centrifuge at 12 000 rpm (13 800 × g) for 1 min. Reload the eluate onto the column and centrifuge a second time to improve DNA recovery yield.

Procedure
  1. Digest the purified target gene insert and empty vector with identical restriction endonucleases according to the digestion system, to generate complementary sticky ends.
  2. Purify the digested insert fragment and vector backbone to remove residual enzymes and impurities.
  3. Prepare the ligation system as shown above, mix gently and incubate the mixture at 22 °C for 2 h for sufficient ligation.
  4. Transform the ligation product into competent cells, followed by plasmid extraction and Sanger sequencing to verify correct recombinant vector construction.

ComponentVolume
5× CE II Buffer1 μL
Exnase II1 μL
Linearized insert fragmentCorresponding volume (molar ratio 2:1)
Linearized vector backboneCorresponding volume (molar ratio 2:1)
Procedure
  1. Prepare the recombination reaction system according to the above table, and pipette gently to mix the components thoroughly.
  2. Incubate the reaction mixture at 37 °C for 30 min for homologous recombination.
  3. After the reaction, place the tube on ice for a short cooling process. Subsequently, perform bacterial transformation, plasmid extraction and Sanger sequencing to verify the correct construction of recombinant vectors.

Note: All reaction systems were prepared on ice to avoid enzyme inactivation. The molar ratio of insert to vector is critical for recombination efficiency and must be strictly controlled. Verified recombinant vectors were stored at −20 °C for subsequent experiments.

2.4 Site‑Directed Mutagenesis

Equipment

  • PCR instrument (Bio‑Rad, USA)
  • Digital display thermostatic water bath (Changzhou Guohua Electric Appliance Co., Ltd., Changzhou, China)
  • Pipettor (Eppendorf, Germany)

Reagents

Site‑directed mutagenesis primers、PCR enzyme mix、DpnI endonuclease、Enzyme reaction buffer、Sterile ddH₂O

DpnI Template Removal Reaction System (50 μL total)

ComponentVolume
Purified PCR recovery product25 μL
DpnI endonuclease1 μL
Enzyme reaction buffer5 μL
Sterile ddH₂OMake up to 50 μL

Procedure

  1. Design site‑directed mutagenesis primers according to the target mutation sites, ensuring that the forward and reverse primers contain at least 20 bp overlapping homologous sequences.
  2. Perform full‑plasmid reverse PCR amplification using sequence‑verified plasmids (pUC57‑Pr‑NahR(E169/C248)‑Psal‑eGFP and pMD18T‑Pnah‑nahA) as templates.
  3. Detect PCR products via agarose gel electrophoresis, recover fragments with correct band size, and purify the mutagenized plasmid products.
  4. Prepare the DpnI template digestion system on ice following the formulated system. After gentle mixing, incubate the reaction system in a thermostatic water bath at 37 °C for 2 h to fully digest methylated wild‑type template plasmids.
  5. Terminate the reaction and inactivate DpnI endonuclease by heating the system at 80 °C for 10 min.
  6. Perform DNA purification and recovery on the enzyme‑treated product, and concentrate the final elution volume to 10 μL.
  7. Take 5 μL of the recovered product to transform Escherichia coli DH5α competent cells, and culture to obtain single transformant colonies.
  8. Select monoclonal transformants for Sanger sequencing verification. Plasmids with accurate target mutation sequences were screened and reserved for subsequent experimental analysis.

2.5 Construction of Dual‑Site Saturated Mutant Library

Equipment

  • PCR instrument (Bio‑Rad, USA)
  • Digital display thermostatic water bath (Changzhou Guohua Electric Appliance Co., Ltd., Changzhou, China)
  • Pipettor (Eppendorf, Germany)
  • Constant temperature incubator (Hengyi, Shanghai, China)

Reagents

Mutagenesis primers、Linearized vector primers、PCR enzyme mix、5× CE II Buffer、Exnase II recombinase、DNA purification kit、Competent Escherichia coli DH5α cells、Sterile deionized water

Procedure

  1. Primer design was performed using SnapGene software. Primers 169‑F and 248‑R were designed for site‑saturation mutagenesis of the 169th and 248th amino acid residues of NahR protein. The NNS degenerate codon was applied to replace the original amino acid codons at the two target sites to achieve full‑site saturation mutation.
  2. The plasmid pUC57‑Pr/Psal‑mut‑NahR(E169/C248)‑eGFP was used as the PCR template. The 169‑F primer containing the 169‑site mutation and the 248‑R primer containing the 248‑site mutation were used for amplification to obtain mutant fragments covering the 169–248 double mutation sites of NahR protein.
  3. PCR products were verified by agarose gel electrophoresis. Fragments with correct band size were purified and recovered using a commercial DNA purification kit.
  4. The recovered mutant fragments and linearized vector (amplified with primers Z‑F and Z‑R) were assembled via homologous recombination. The reaction system was prepared with an insert‑to‑vector molar ratio of 2:1, supplemented with 5× CE II Buffer and Exnase II, and incubated at 37 °C for 30 min to complete recombination ligation.
  5. The recombination product was directly transformed into competent Escherichia coli DH5α cells. The transformed plates were incubated overnight at 37 °C in an inverted state.
  6. Statistical analysis showed that approximately 1400 transformants were obtained for the NahR saturation mutant library, which was more than three times the theoretical mutation number of 400, indicating that the library covered all theoretical mutation combinations.
  7. Ten transformants were randomly selected from the library plates under a sterile environment. Monoclonal strains were cultured and plasmids were extracted for Sanger sequencing verification.
  8. Sequencing results demonstrated that 9 out of 10 selected transformants carried different amino acid mutations at both the 169th and 248th sites of NahR protein, confirming high mutation efficiency and successful construction of the saturated mutant library.

3.1 Preparation of Escherichia coli DH5α Competent Cells

Equipment

  • Constant temperature incubator (Laiboteri, Tianjin, China)
  • Constant temperature shaker (Ounuotest, Tianjin, China)
  • High‑speed refrigerated centrifuge (Dragon‑Lab, Beijing, China)
  • Pipettor (Eppendorf, Germany)
  • Ultra‑low temperature refrigerator (Haier, China)

Reagents

Escherichia coli DH5α frozen strain、Sterile LB liquid medium、Sterile 0.1 mol/L CaCl₂ solution、Sterile 70% glycerol

Procedure

  1. Strain revival: Take out the frozen stock of Escherichia coli DH5α from the −80 °C ultra‑low temperature refrigerator. Streak the bacterial strain on a sterile LB solid agar plate using a sterile inoculating loop. Incubate the plate in an inverted state at 37 °C for 12–16 h overnight to obtain single monoclonal colonies.
  2. Seed culture preparation: Pick a well‑grown single colony and inoculate it into 5 mL sterile LB liquid medium. Culture the bacteria at 37 °C with continuous shaking at 200 rpm for 12–16 h to prepare saturated seed culture.
  3. Scale‑up culture: Inoculate 1 mL of the seed culture into 100 mL sterile LB liquid medium at a dilution ratio of 1:100. Incubate at 37 °C and 200 rpm for 2–3 h. Continuously monitor the OD₆₀₀ value, and terminate the culture when the value reaches 0.4–0.6 to obtain bacteria in the early logarithmic phase with high cell competence.
  4. Ice‑bath pretreatment: Quickly transfer the cultured bacterial suspension into pre‑chilled sterile centrifuge tubes, and place the tubes on ice for 30 min to slowly cool the bacterial liquid and inhibit cell metabolism.
  5. Cell harvest: Centrifuge the bacterial suspension at 4 °C and 3700 rpm for 10 min. Discard the supernatant completely and retain the cell pellet at the bottom of the tube. All operations are performed on ice to prevent the loss of cell competence caused by temperature rise.
  6. Cell washing: Add 10 mL of pre‑chilled 0.1 mol/L sterile CaCl₂ solution to the centrifuge tube, and gently resuspend the cell pellet to avoid cell lysis caused by violent shaking. Place the tube on ice for 10 min, then centrifuge at 4 °C and 3700 rpm for 10 min, and discard the supernatant. Repeat the washing operation 2–3 times.
  7. Concentrated resuspension: According to the amount of cell pellet, add 1.6 mL pre‑chilled 0.1 mol/L sterile CaCl₂ solution and 0.4 mL sterile 70% glycerol to fully resuspend the cells. Place the mixture on ice for 5 min to obtain uniform competent cell suspension.
  8. Aliquot and storage: Dispense the prepared competent cell suspension into pre‑chilled sterile centrifuge tubes with 100 μL per tube. The prepared competent cells can be used directly for recombinant product transformation, or stored long‑term in a −80 °C ultra‑low temperature refrigerator for subsequent experiments.

3.2 Chemical Transformation of Competent Cells

Equipment

  • Digital display thermostatic water bath (Changzhou Guohua Electric Appliance Co., Ltd., Changzhou, China)
  • Constant temperature shaker (Tianjin Honour Instrument Co., Ltd., Tianjin, China)
  • High‑speed centrifuge (Dragon‑Lab, Beijing, China)
  • Pipettor (Eppendorf, Germany)
  • Constant temperature incubator (Labotery, Tianjin, China)
  • Ultra‑clean workbench (Suzhou Antai Airtech Co., Ltd., Suzhou, China)

Reagents

Escherichia coli DH5α competent cells、Homologous recombination products、Sterile LB liquid medium、LK solid agar plate containing kanamycin、75% ethanol

Procedure

  1. Sample preparation: Thaw one aliquot of freshly prepared or −80 °C frozen DH5α competent cells naturally on ice. Avoid repeated kneading of the tube during thawing to prevent cell damage caused by abrupt temperature changes.
  2. Mixing of recombinant product and competent cells: After complete thawing of competent cells, slowly add 5 μL of homologous recombination product into the competent cell suspension. Mix gently with a pipette without harsh blowing and pipetting. Place the mixture on ice for 30 min for steady combination.
  3. Heat‑shock treatment: Vertically place the centrifuge tube into a 42 °C thermostatic water bath and perform accurate heat shock for 45 s to induce transient permeability of cell membrane.
  4. Ice‑bath recovery: Immediately transfer the tube back to ice after heat shock and incubate for 2 min to promote rapid contraction of the cell membrane and stabilize the adsorption of exogenous DNA.
  5. Post‑transformation recovery culture: Add 200 μL of sterile LB liquid medium to the tube and mix gently. Incubate at 37 °C with shaking at 200 rpm for 45 min to restore the metabolic activity of transformed cells.
  6. Plating operation: Centrifuge the recovered bacterial culture at 10 000 rpm for 1 min. In an ultra‑clean workbench, discard partial supernatant and retain 50 μL of liquid to fully resuspend the cell pellet. Evenly spread the bacterial suspension on the kanamycin‑resistant LB (LK) solid plate using a sterile spreader which was pre‑sterilized with 75% ethanol and flame cooling.
  7. Culture incubation: Place the plated medium in an inverted state and incubate at 37 °C for 12–16 h overnight. The cultured plates with uniform and appropriate‑sized single colonies can be stored at 4 °C for subsequent experiments.

3.3 Colony PCR Verification

Equipment

  • PCR instrument, Bio‑Rad, USA
  • Pipettor (Eppendorf, Germany)
  • Ultra‑clean workbench (Suzhou Antai Airtech Co., Ltd., Suzhou, China)
  • Gel electrophoresis apparatus (Beijing Liuyi Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China)
  • Gel imaging system (Beijing Liuyi Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China)

Reagents

PCR master mix, identification primers, sterile dd H₂O, TAE buffer, agarose gel, nucleic acid dye, selective LB medium

PCR Reaction System (10 μL total)

ComponentVolume
PCR master mix5 μL
Forward primer0.5 μL
Reverse primer0.5 μL
Bacterial suspension template1 μL
Sterile ddH₂OFill up to 10 μL

Procedure

  1. After the transformation of ligation or recombination products into Escherichia coli competent cells, colony PCR was performed to preliminarily verify recombinant colonies carrying target DNA fragments.
  2. In an ultra‑clean workbench, ten single colonies were randomly picked and separately resuspended in 10 μL sterile ddH₂O with clear labeling to serve as PCR templates. A positive colony containing the target fragment and a negative colony without insertion were set as control groups.
  3. Prepare the 10 μL PCR reaction system according to the table above. After fully mixing, place the sample in a PCR instrument and set appropriate thermal cycling parameters for amplification.
  4. The PCR products were detected by agarose gel electrophoresis. Colonies with band sizes consistent with the positive control and without non‑specific bands were regarded as positive recombinants.
  5. Qualified positive colonies were inoculated into corresponding selective liquid medium and cultured for 8–12 h. The bacterial cultures were collected for plasmid extraction and subsequent Sanger sequencing verification.
IV. Screening System for Mutant Strains

Mutant Strains Screening Procedure

Screening System for Mutant Strains

Equipment

  • Pipettor (Eppendorf, Germany)
  • Ultra‑clean workbench (Suzhou Antai Airtech Co., Ltd., Suzhou, China)
  • Constant temperature incubator (Labotery, Tianjin, China)
  • Constant temperature shaker (Tianjin Honour Instrument Co., Ltd., Tianjin, China)
  • Blue‑light imager (Beijing Liuyi Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China)
  • Microplate reader (Molecular Devices, USA)

Reagents

Sterile deionized water、Sterile LB solid medium、Sterile LB liquid medium、Salicylic acid

Procedure

  1. All transformants on the solid agar plate were washed with a suitable volume of sterile water using a sterile spreader to obtain a uniform pooled mutant library cell suspension.
  2. The cell suspension was diluted to an appropriate concentration with sterile water and evenly spread on LB agar plates containing 0.1 mM salicylic acid.
  3. The plated plates were incubated upside down at 37 °C overnight to obtain monoclonal colonies of mutant strains.
  4. Primary screening was performed using a blue‑light imager. Colonies with significantly enhanced fluorescence intensity were preliminarily selected as candidate mutant strains.
  5. The screened candidate colonies were spot‑purified on fresh antibiotic‑free solid medium to eliminate mixed colonies and obtain pure monoclonal strains.
  6. Single colonies obtained from primary screening were picked and inoculated into sterile LB liquid medium supplemented with 1 μM salicylic acid for induced culture. The strains were incubated at 37 °C with shaking at 220 rpm for 10 h.
  7. A microplate reader was used to quantitatively detect the fluorescence intensity of all primary screening candidates for secondary screening. Mutant strains with low background fluorescence signal and high sensing sensitivity were finally selected for subsequent experimental verification.
V. Salicylic Acid Induction and Sensing Performance Assay

Induction & Sensing Performance Assay

Salicylic Acid Induction and Sensing Performance Assay

Equipment

  • Pipettor (Eppendorf, Germany)
  • Ultra‑clean workbench (Suzhou Antai Airtech Co., Ltd., Suzhou, China)
  • Constant temperature shaker (Tianjin Honour Instrument Co., Ltd., Tianjin, China)
  • Microplate reader (Molecular Devices, USA)

Reagents

Escherichia coli DH5α competent cells、Sterile LB liquid medium、LK < DH5α competent cells、Sterile LB liquid medium、LK selective medium、Salicylic acid、DMSO

Procedure

  1. The salicylic acid detecting strains were constructed by transforming the sensing plasmid pUC57‑NahR‑eGFP into Escherichia coli DH5α competent cells.
  2. A single positive colony was picked and inoculated into 1 mL sterile LB liquid medium for overnight seed culture at 37 °C for 15 h.
  3. A total of 20 μL overnight seed culture was inoculated into 20 mL LK selective medium, and three independent biological replicates were set for the experiment. The cultures were incubated at 37 °C with shaking at 220 rpm.
  4. When the OD₆₀₀ value of the bacterial culture reached approximately 0.3, salicylic acid inducer was added to the experimental groups. The control group (0 μM salicylic acid) was supplemented with an equal volume of DMSO solvent to eliminate solvent interference.
  5. All bacterial cultures were continuously incubated at 37 °C and 220 rpm for 8 h to fully induce intracellular eGFP fluorescent signal expression.
  6. After induction, the microplate reader was used to detect the fluorescence intensity at excitation wavelength of 488 nm and emission wavelength of 511 nm, as well as the OD₆₀₀ absorbance value of each sample. The relative fluorescence value was calculated as FL/OD₆₀₀ to eliminate the influence of bacterial concentration difference.

6.1 Resting‑Cell Preparation and Degradation Assay

Equipment

  • Pipettor (Eppendorf, Germany)
  • Ultra‑clean workbench (Suzhou Antai Airtech Co., Ltd., Suzhou, China)
  • Constant temperature shaker (Tianjin Honour Instrument Co., Ltd., Tianjin, China)
  • High‑speed centrifuge (Dragon‑Lab, Beijing, China)

Reagents

Degradation‑engineered strain、Sterile LB liquid medium、Corresponding antibiotic、Basal salt medium、Naphthalene

Procedure

  1. A fresh single colony of the degradation‑engineered strain was picked and inoculated into sterile LB liquid medium containing the corresponding antibiotic. The strain was cultured at 37 °C with shaking at 220 rpm until the OD₆₀₀ value reached approximately 2.0.
  2. The bacterial culture was centrifuged to harvest cell pellets. The collected cells were washed twice with fresh basal salt medium to remove residual medium components.
  3. The cleaned cell pellets were resuspended in basal salt medium and adjusted to a final OD₆₀₀ of 4.0 for subsequent resting‑cell degradation assay.
  4. Naphthalene was added into the bacterial suspension to a final concentration of 100 μM. The mixture was incubated at 37 °C with continuous shaking to initiate and perform the naphthalene degradation experiment.

6.2 HPLC Detection Conditions for Naphthalene

Equipment

  • High performance liquid chromatography (Shimadzu, Japan)
  • Ultrasonic cleaner (Kun Shan Ultrasonic Instruments Co., Ltd., Jiangsu, China)
  • High‑speed centrifuge (Dragon‑Lab, Beijing, China)
  • Pipettor (Eppendorf, Germany)

Reagents

HPLC‑grade methanol、Glacial acetic acid、Deionized water、Naphthalene degradation samples

Procedure

  1. High‑performance liquid chromatography (HPLC, Shimadzu, Japan) was applied for the quantitative detection of naphthalene and its metabolic products. The chromatographic detection parameters were set as follows: InertSustain AQ‑C18 chromatographic column (4.6 × 250 mm); mobile phase consisting of 90% HPLC‑grade methanol and 10% deionized water supplemented with 1% glacial acetic acid; flow rate of 0.5 mL/min; detection wavelength of 280 nm; injection volume of 5–20 μL.
  2. Prior to detection, the treated degradation sample was mixed with an equal volume of HPLC‑grade methanol. The mixture was ultrasonicated for 10 min, followed by centrifugation. The clarified supernatant was collected and used for HPLC injection and detection.

6.3 HPLC Detection Conditions for Salicylic Acid

Equipment

  • High performance liquid chromatography (Shimadzu, Japan)
  • Ultrasonic cleaner (Kun Shan Ultrasonic Instruments Co., Ltd., Jiangsu, China)
  • High‑speed centrifuge (Dragon‑Lab, Beijing, China)
  • Pipettor (Eppendorf, Germany)

Reagents

HPLC‑grade acetonitrile、Trifluoroacetic acid (TFA)、Deionized water、Salicylic acid test samples

Procedure

  1. High‑performance liquid chromatography was used for the quantitative determination of salicylic acid. The detection parameters were set as follows: InertSustain AQ‑C18 chromatographic column (4.6 × 250 mm); mobile phase composed of 40% HPLC‑grade acetonitrile and 60% deionized water containing 0.3% trifluoroacetic acid (TFA); flow rate of 0.8 mL/min; detection wavelength of 285 nm; conventional injection volume of 5–20 μL.
  2. Before detection, the sample was mixed with an equal volume of HPLC‑grade acetonitrile, ultrasonicated for 10 min, and centrifuged to collect the supernatant for HPLC injection and quantitative analysis.
VII. ARTP Mutagenesis

ARTP Mutagenesis Workflow

ARTP Mutagenesis

Equipment

  • ARTP mutagenesis system (Taimu Biological, Wuxi, China)
  • Constant temperature shaker (Ounuotest, Tianjin, China)
  • Constant temperature incubator (Labotery, Tianjin, China)

Reagents

LB medium powder、Kanamycin、50% glycerol、Sterile water、75% ethanol、Experimental strain:MUT2nd(G169T248)、LK medium

Procedure

1. Strain activation and cultivation
  1. Strain activation: The MUT2nd mutant strain preserved at -80 °C was streaked on LK agar plates and incubated upside down at 37 °C for 12–16 h (overnight) to obtain single colonies.
  2. Seed culture: A plump single colony was picked and inoculated into 5 mL liquid LK medium. The culture was shaken at 37 °C and 200 rpm for 2–3 h until the OD₆₀₀ reached 0.4–0.6 to prepare the seed culture.
2. Preparations before ARTP mutagenesis
  1. Sterilize the metal slides for ARTP treatment to guarantee an aseptic experimental environment.
  2. Inside the ultra‑clean workbench, wipe the working surface, surrounding area and inner wall with wrung ethanol cotton swabs. Turn on UV light for 15–30 min for sterilization.
3. ARTP mutagenesis treatment
  1. Sample spreading: 10 μL seed culture was spread evenly onto the surface of a sterilized ARTP metal slide.
  2. Parameter setup
    • a. Follow the standard startup procedure: switch on the main unit, cooling circulator and gas cylinder. Adjust the grey knob of the gas cylinder and tighten the blue knob until the pressure gauge reads 0.15–0.2 MPa.
    • b. Mutagenesis parameters: gas flow 10 SLM, power 120 W, exposure time 40 s; the 0 s group was set as control.
  3. Mutagenesis run: Place the slide with bacterial suspension at the treatment position of the ARTP instrument, and perform mutagenesis according to the preset duration.
  4. Instrument shutdown: After treatment, shut down following the standard procedure: turn off the gas supply first, then power. Close the grey knob of the gas cylinder; switch off the flowmeter after the gas reading drops to zero, and loosen the blue knob.Wipe the workbench, surrounding area and inner wall with wrung ethanol cotton swabs, then irradiate with UV light for 15–20 min. Switch off the cooling circulator and the main instrument.
4. Post‑mutagenesis sample processing and cultivation
  1. Cell elution: After plasma treatment, immerse the slide into a test tube containing 1 mL sterile water and vortex for 2 min to resuspend bacterial cells.
  2. Inoculate the bacterial suspension into 10 mL LK medium and incubate at 37 °C, 200 rpm for 1.5 h for post‑mutagenesis recovery, to restore cell activity, reduce cell death and facilitate subsequent screening.
  3. Strain preservation: Mix the recovered culture with 50% glycerol at a volume ratio of 1:1, transfer into sterile cryotubes and store at -80 °C.
